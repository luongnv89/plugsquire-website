FAQ

Questions about Plugsquire

What does Plugsquire send over the internet?

With "Online details" on, a public model name and a maker name go to the USB ID Repository, Wikipedia and Wikidata, and Apple Support. The security check sends product names and your macOS and Safari version numbers to the National Vulnerability Database, and an update check asks this website for the list of versions. Serial numbers, Bluetooth addresses, volume names and the names you give devices never leave your Mac.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs running macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon and Intel. Spotting power-only devices on USB-C ports needs an Apple silicon Mac.

Does it need admin rights or a helper?

No. It uses no root access, no private APIs and no helper daemon. Full Disk Access is optional and asked for only when a feature needs it: without it, the Trash and iPhone backups show as "Not measured".

Will cleaning delete something I need?

Nothing is removed until you confirm the exact list, and every item is checked again just before it goes. Xcode archives, iPhone backups, large files and uninstalled apps go to the Trash, so you can put them back. Cleaning stays out of Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Pictures, iCloud and Mail.

Does it scan my network's ports?

Only when you ask. The local network screen listens to what devices announce. The open-port check runs on one device at a time, after you confirm a warning, and only on a private address inside your own Wi-Fi or Ethernet network.

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

The App Store sandbox blocks the hardware reads Plugsquire depends on, so it is distributed outside the store.

Is Pro a subscription?

No. Pro is a one-time $14.99 payment for the version you buy and the updates published for it.
